<p>The vulnerability reading holds at 0.91 for another month, anchored by the same structural conditions — a CAPE percentile near the top of its historical range, credit spreads compressed to the 98th percentile, and margin-loan leverage elevated well above year-ago levels — while the trigger composite has moved modestly higher to 0.42, a change worth noting even if it does not yet constitute an ignition signal. The shift in the trigger reflects a mild uptick in the volatility-rising gauge (now 0.48) and a modest deterioration in price momentum (0.47), both of which have crossed the midpoint for the first time in several months; spread widening remains subdued at 0.22, so the three ignition gauges are not moving in concert, and no active stress cascade appears to be under way.</p>

<p>The state therefore remains Elevated rather than Critical: the tinder is as dry as it has been all year, and the spark gauges are registering slightly more friction than last month, but friction is not fire. The qualitative risk the quantitative framework remains least equipped to capture has evolved from last month's note: beyond the AI-linked credit concentration flagged previously, financial conditions (NFCI at the 29th percentile) have not tightened meaningfully despite the momentum gauge beginning to edge higher, and a rapid, disorderly tightening — whether driven by a policy surprise, a sovereign credit event, or a sudden reassessment of terminal-rate assumptions — could compress the lag between the current vulnerability reading and an active trigger in ways the score would register only after the fact. Nothing in the current gauges signals that such a sequence is imminent, but the combination of peak-range valuations, thin credit risk premia, and elevated margin leverage means the margin for absorption of an external shock is narrower than historical norms would suggest.</p>
