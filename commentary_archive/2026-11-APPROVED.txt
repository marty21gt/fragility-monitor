The trigger composite has moved up to 0.47 this month, its highest reading in the current cycle, driven by a further rise in the volatility-rising gauge (now 0.55) and continued softening in price momentum (0.44); spread widening has also picked up to 0.38, meaning all three ignition gauges are now moving in the same direction for the first time, even if none has crossed into territory that would characterize an active stress cascade. Vulnerability remains anchored at 0.91 — still the dominant feature of the dashboard — with CAPE at the 99th percentile, credit spreads compressed to the 98th percentile, and margin-loan leverage at the 89th percentile, conditions that collectively describe a system carrying significant embedded fragility. The state therefore remains Elevated rather than Critical: the change from last month is that the spark gauges are no longer drifting in isolation but are beginning to move together, a shift in character worth watching even though ignition has not occurred. The qualitative risk the quantitative framework is least equipped to capture at this reading is concentration in the credit-intermediation chain around a small number of large counterparties with correlated AI-related exposure — a structure in which a single idiosyncratic credit event, particularly in leveraged lending or private credit vehicles, could transmit rapidly through collateral chains before spread-widening gauges have time to respond. Compounding that, the conditions-tightening context gauge has risen to 0.69, suggesting that the financial-conditions cushion that absorbed earlier friction may be narrowing, even as the headline NFCI reading remains relatively loose at 0.41. Nothing in the current data indicates that a drawdown is under way, but the convergence of the trigger sub-components — against a vulnerability backdrop that has shown no material relief — represents a more textured picture of ambient risk than was present a month ago.
