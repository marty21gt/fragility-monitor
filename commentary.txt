The vulnerability reading holds at 0.91 this month, sustained by the same structural conditions noted last month — a CAPE near 41, a Baa–Aaa credit spread compressed to 0.48%, and margin-loan leverage running 28% above year-ago levels — with no meaningful change in any of those underlying gauges; the system remains primed to the same unusual degree, but that alone does not constitute an alarm. What has shifted, modestly, is the trigger side: all three ignition gauges — spread widening, rising volatility, and price momentum deterioration — continue to read below 0.30, and none shows evidence of active stress taking hold, leaving the state at Elevated rather than Critical. The gap between an extremely fragile structure and a still-quiet trigger is the defining feature of this reading, and that gap can persist for an extended period without resolving into a drawdown.


The qualitative risk the quantitative framework is least equipped to capture remains concentrated in AI-linked credit: specifically, the possibility that a cluster of data-center or infrastructure borrowers encounters simultaneous covenant stress or a ratings action at a moment when secondary-market liquidity for that paper is thin — a dynamic that could cause credit spreads to gap wider before the momentum gauges register it as a trend. Financial conditions remain relatively loose by the NFCI measure, which means the tightening impulse, if it arrives, would be starting from a position where markets have not yet priced in much caution. As of this reading, nothing in the scored gauges is igniting; the note is a description of ambient fragility, not a signal of imminent dislocation.
